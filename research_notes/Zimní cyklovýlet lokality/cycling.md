# Road-cycling quality of Jan–May destinations (southern Europe, Canaries, Madeira, Turkey, Morocco)

Context: a group of 5–6 Czech road riders who already know Mallorca, Tenerife and Gran Canaria. They want 4–5 nights between Jan and Apr/May 2027, flying from Prague or Vienna and renting bikes on site.

Method note: I did 21 web searches. Most sources are cycling-tour operators, Komoot, PJAMM, Climbfinder and race previews. Climb figures differ between sources depending on start point, and I note the conflicts where they appear. Some things I could not source within the budget: traffic and tunnels on Madeira, dogs in Turkey, Crete and Morocco, Tuscany winter conditions, and current pro-team camp lists for 2026/27. Those are listed under Gaps. All 1–5 scores are my own inferences, not sourced ratings.

## Q1: Location profiles and scores (roads, quietness, climbing, infrastructure, variety)

### Takeaway
For riders who know Mallorca and the western Canaries, the strongest "new" road destinations are Costa Blanca (Calpe/Dénia), Cyprus (Troodos), La Palma, Andalusia (Almería/Granada, plus Ronda/Málaga) and the Algarve. Lanzarote and Fuerteventura are rideable but low, windy and repetitive. Girona and Tuscany are better from April/May. Madeira and Morocco are adventurous options for a strong group.

### Cited Findings

**General context**
- Recurring European winter-camp destinations: Mallorca, the Canaries, Costa Blanca, the Algarve and Andalucía. Girona, Sicily/Etna and Malta appear less often — [Rouvy](https://rouvy.com/blog/best-cycling-holidays-europe); [eatsleepcycle](https://www.eatsleepcycle.com/european-winter-cycling-destinations/); [bicyclesandcycling](https://www.bicyclesandcycling.com/winter-cycling-destinations-in-europe/)
- Pro winter camps are concentrated in Costa Blanca. Mallorca remains INEOS's destination. A Sports Beans-Wilier camp was held in Lanjarón (Sierra Nevada), and Trek has used "southern Spain" for its January camp — [brujulabike](https://en.brujulabike.com/spain-favourite-destination-professional-cycling-teams/); [Cycling Weekly](https://cyclingweekly.com/news/latest-news/sports-beans-in-spain-70294); [Trek racing](https://racing.trekbikes.com/stories/trek-segafredo-men/january-training-camp-in-images-day-2)

**Lanzarote**
- Climbs: Tabayesco is about 10 km. Femés is very steep, with the final metres "well over 20%". Mirador del Río from Órzola is 8.6 km and about 470 m of gain, the highest road point on the island. The Tabayesco road was once closed to bikes and reopened by March 2023, so check current status — [tour-magazin](https://www.tour-magazin.de/en/tours/spain/lanzarote-four-dream-road-bike-tours-on-the-canary-island/); [La Voz de Lanzarote](https://www.lavozdelanzarote.com/en/things-to-do-in-lanzarote/the-best-road-biking-routes-in-lanzarote_213715_102.html); [Komoot](https://www.komoot.com/highlight/3226999); [BikeRadar forum](https://forum.bikeradar.com/discussion/12679558/lanzarote)
- Wind: the constant NE trade wind passes over the low relief. Forum riders report headwinds strong enough that they "could barely move forward" — [BikeRadar forum](https://forum.bikeradar.com/discussion/12951606/lanzarote); [muchbetteradventures](https://www.muchbetteradventures.com/magazine/winter-cycling-destinations-europe/)

**Fuerteventura**
- Interior loops via Betancuria are 75–96 km with about 1,300 m of gain. The FV-30 climb is about 5.9 km with about 210 m gain, moderate and exposed. There are "no extreme climbs like those on Gran Canaria or Tenerife" — [Komoot](https://www.komoot.com/highlight/7097927); [fuerteventuraguide](https://www.fuerteventuraguide.com/?p=409)
- The trade wind can make a 90 km ride "a far harder day than the elevation profile suggests". 28–30 mm tyres are recommended. The best season is Oct–Apr — [procyclinguk](https://procyclinguk.com/cycling-in-fuerteventura-why-it-deserves-a-place-on-your-riding-list/)

**La Palma**
- Roque de los Muchachos (east, from Santa Cruz): about 41 km and about 2,470 m gain at a 5.6% average. Passzwang says ">2,500 m over >40 km at ~7%". The first 32 km average 6.8%, followed by a 5.3 km false descent and a final 3.9 km at 7.3% with pitches to about 12%. The summit is 2,426 m. Fog, cloud and occasional snow are hazards. The road is paved throughout (LP-4) — [PJAMM](https://pjammcycling.com/climb/428.Roque%20de%20Los%20Muchacho); [Passzwang](https://www.passzwang.net/en/roquemuchachos_cycling.php)
- West side: about 28.5 km (17.7 mi) and about 2,275 m (7,465 ft) at a 7.4% average, starting from Mirador el Serradero — [PJAMM](https://pjammcycling.com/climb/5321.Roque-de-los-Muchachos-west)

**Costa Blanca (Calpe/Dénia)**
- Signature climbs are Coll de Rates and Vall d'Ebo. Calpe has been the winter base for the majority of WorldTour pre-season camps. An older claim says 16 of 18 WorldTeams are based there. Pros cluster in Dec–Jan and amateur groups peak Feb–Apr. Aranburu called Coll de Rates "like a motorway for cyclists" — [Rouvy press](https://rouvy.com/en/press/ROUVY-training-camp-2026); [Yahoo/Cycling](https://uk.sports.yahoo.com/news/welcome-costa-blanca-pro-cyclings-065647981.html); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/lifestyle/travel-leisure/article/3295064/how-spains-costa-blanca-has-become-cycling-hotspot-winter); [Cycling Weekly guide](https://www.cyclingweekly.com/routes/overseas/cycling-denia-calpe-411675)
- Calpe is about 1 hour from Alicante airport. Dénia has a pro-favourite recovery-oriented hotel — [eatsleepcycle](https://www.eatsleepcycle.com/european-winter-cycling-destinations/)

**Girona / Costa Brava**
- Winter temperatures are 5–15 °C. March–May is the ideal season (15–25 °C) and has the highest density of resident pros. Some sources say visitors mostly skip winter — [ctyeh](https://ctyeh.com/articles/1499?lang=en); [GCN](https://content.globalcyclingnetwork.com/lifestyle/adventure/ultimate-guide-to-cycling-in-girona)
- Rocacorba: 9.9 km at 7% or 13.8 km at 6.5% (sources conflict), paved in 2006. Els Àngels: about 12 km at 4.5% from Madremanya; another source says 3.5% — [GCN](https://content.globalcyclingnetwork.com/lifestyle/adventure/ultimate-guide-to-cycling-in-girona); [Roadman](https://roadmancycling.com/blog/girona-cycling-guide); [Laka](https://laka.co/gb/blog/road-bike/laka-rides-in-spain-the-ultimate-travel-guide-to-the-lanes-of-girona)

**Andalusia**
- Almería: Alto de Velefique is 13.1 km at 7.2%, with 23 hairpins in an 11 km section at 7.7%. The road is in excellent condition with no shoulder. Calar Alto is 16.9 km at 5.6% to about 2,100 m, a quiet dead-end road to the observatory that is wind-exposed at altitude. Both were used on Vuelta 2026 stage 12 (Vera–Calar Alto) — [Cyclingnews](https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/racing/vuelta-a-espana-2026-stage-12-preview/); [PJAMM Velefique](https://pjammcycling.com/climb/2707.Alto-Velefique); [domestiquecycling](https://www.domestiquecycling.com/en/news/vuelta-a-espana-stage-12-preview-high-altitude-finish-to-shake-up-the-gc/)
- Granada: Pico Veleta is about 31 km (19.4 mi) and about 2,400 m (7,875 ft) at a 7.7% average. The last 11 km are closed to motor vehicles. The final rocky ~2 km near 3,300 m is not suitable for road bikes. I found no source on winter closures, but snow above the ski station in Jan–Apr is very likely (see Inferences) — [PJAMM](https://pjammcycling.com/climb/5.Pico%20Veleta&sa=D&ust=1571186826389000); [Cycling UK](https://www.cyclinguk.org/cycle-magazine/great-rides-pico-de-veleta-spain)
- Ronda/Málaga: Puerto del Viento from El Burgo is 13.3 km, 612 m, 4.6%, summit about 1,190 m. Traffic is light and the surface good. A loop from Ronda is about 78 km with 1,450 m — [Komoot](https://www.komoot.com/smarttour/e939776107/ascent-to-puerto-del-viento-circular-from-ronda); [Skedaddle](https://www.skedaddle.com/uk/classicroad/holiday/Road_Cycling/Spain_-_Roads_of_Ronda_-_Guided_Road_Cycling_Holiday/484/view.rails)

**Cyprus**
- Mount Olympus from Trimiklini: 21.4 km, 1,416 m, 6.6%, steepest km about 15%. From Limassol at sea level it is about 1,900 m over about 50 km. The summit area has military radar, so the road ends at a checkpoint — [PJAMM](https://pjammcycling.com/climb/3817.Mount-Olympus-Cyprus-%28Trimiklini%29); [BikeRadar forum](https://forum.bikeradar.com/discussion/12582441/cycling-in-cyprus); [Sportive Breaks](https://sportivebreaks.com/cycling-climbs/mt-olympus/)
- Traffic drives on the left, but oncoming traffic is unlikely on many side roads. Inland roads are very quiet but surfaces can be rough, with potholes on side-road descents. The main season is mid-Feb to early May. Snow is possible around 2,000 m in winter, and brief winter and spring snow closures were reported in 2026 — [Cycling UK](https://www.cyclinguk.org/cycle/great-rides-cyprus-season); [tour-magazin](https://www.tour-magazin.de/en/europe/road-bike-training-camp-in-spring-start-of-the-season-in-cyprus/); [responsibletravel](https://www.responsibletravel.com/holidays/cyprus-cycling/travel-guide); [PJAMM](https://pjammcycling.com/climb/3817.Mount-Olympus-Cyprus-%28Trimiklini%29)

**Madeira**
- Pico do Arieiro is "widely considered the hardest bike climb in Portugal". The easier line is about 21.4 km (13.3 mi) at 8.3%. Rouvy lists 15.95 km at 8.3% with a maximum of 19.6%. From Funchal it is 44.8 km with 1,870 m. Passzwang calls it the steepest of Macaronesia's four big volcanic climbs. The ER202 is the main access road — [PJAMM](https://pjammcycling.com/climb/1071.Eira-Do-Serrado---Easier-Route); [Passzwang](https://www.passzwang.net/en/picoarieiro_cycling.php); [Rouvy](https://rouvy.com/it/all-rouvy-routes/94868); [Climbfinder](https://climbfinder.com/en/regions/madeira)

**Algarve**
- Fóia from Caldas de Monchique: 16.4 km, 795 m, 4.9%, maximum 11.1%, summit 896 m. The Volta ao Algarve 2025 finish was 7.4 km at 5.6%, with the north side including a 3.5 km section at about 10% — [Climbfinder](https://climbfinder.com/en/climbs/foia-monchique); [cyclingstage](https://www.cyclingstage.com/volta-ao-algarve-2025/stage-2-route-vaa-2025/)
- Quiet backroads and hills, plus sandy doubletrack for gravel riding — [muchbetteradventures](https://www.muchbetteradventures.com/magazine/winter-cycling-destinations-europe/)

**Sicily (Etna)**
- Nicolosi–Rifugio Sapienza: about 18 km at 6.3–6.6%, 1,073–1,187 m gain, to about 1,900 m. The summit is a car park and ski lifts, which inrng calls a "bit of a disappointment". I found no winter-specific info — [inrng](https://inrng.com/?p=31088); [Giro d'Italia](https://www.giroditalia.it/en/ciclovie/the-climbs-through-the-etna-park-ragalna-rifugio-sapienza/)

**Crete**
- Lasithi Plateau sits at about 850 m and the climb to it is rated hard. Snow covers the plateau in winter. A Sissi–Elounda loop is about 61 km with 1,110 m. Secondary roads are generally good, but watch for potholes on steep slopes. There are livestock gates — [Edelweiss](https://www.flyedelweiss.com/at/fr/destinations/tips/road-cycling/crete.html); [Pinkbike blog](https://www.pinkbike.com/u/apperstudio/blog/discovering-crete.html); [Komoot](https://www.komoot.com/smarttour/1259185)

**Antalya / Turkish riviera**
- Tour of Antalya is an early-season UCI race that draws some WorldTeams (Bahrain, Alpecin-Deceuninck in 2024). The peloton once met oncoming traffic on a dual carriageway, which points to weak road-closure discipline — [Hürriyet](https://hurriyetdailynews.com/5th-tour-of-antalya-kicks-off-190548); [Stickybottle](https://www.stickybottle.com/races-results/major-mistake-as-pro-race-forced-to-switch-across-grass-verge-due-to-traffic-video-130560)
- Back roads inland can be rough with few supplies (2007 account, dated) — [travellingtwo](https://travellingtwo.com/442)

**Marrakech / High Atlas**
- Oukaïmeden is the serious climb near Marrakech, rising to a ski area at over 2,000 m, exposed higher up. Tizi n'Tichka (2,260 m) is 35 km at only about 3% average, 104 km from Marrakech. Roadworks can make the first part unrideable, so a transfer may be needed. It is usually done as a 4–6 day loop. Best season: late Feb–early May — [procyclinguk](https://procyclinguk.com/cycling-in-the-high-atlas-from-marrakech-the-climbs-roads-and-atmosphere-that-make-it-special/); [Sportive Breaks](https://sportivebreaks.com/cycling-climbs/tizi-n-tichka/); [Cycling UK](https://www.cyclinguk.org/cycle-magazine/great-rides-crossing-atlas)

**Tuscany**
- April–May climate is described as 19–28 °C, a spring rather than winter destination — [muchbetteradventures](https://www.muchbetteradventures.com/magazine/winter-cycling-destinations-europe/)

**Comparison: Mallorca, Tenerife, Gran Canaria**
- Mallorca: 12–15 °C in Jan–Feb, with possible rain or snow. Pro teams train there Dec–Feb. The Canaries are the most popular winter-training spot (Teide for altitude) — [eatsleepcycle](https://www.eatsleepcycle.com/european-winter-cycling-destinations/); [Rouvy](https://rouvy.com/blog/best-cycling-holidays-europe)

- **Best "new" alternatives for this group:**
  1. Costa Blanca: the closest experience to Mallorca, with the best rental and hotel infrastructure and reliable Jan–Apr weather. Easy flights to Alicante from Prague or Vienna are likely, but check.
  2. Cyprus (Mar–Apr): a fresh place with big climbs and quiet roads. Left-hand traffic needs a briefing.
  3. Almería, combined with Granada if the dates are March or later: Vuelta-grade climbs on empty roads.
  4. La Palma: a "harder Tenerife", but logistics are harder. It likely needs a connection via Madrid or Tenerife.
  5. Algarve: the easiest option for a mixed-ability group, with good gravel.
- A mixed-ability group of 5–6 suits places with flat or rolling options alongside climbs: Costa Blanca, Algarve, Girona, Cyprus. Madeira (constant 10–20% gradients) and La Palma (every ride is mountainous) are hard on weaker riders unless e-road bikes are rented.
- Expected snow at altitude: Calar Alto (2,100 m), Olympus (about 1,900 m) and Etna (1,900 m) can be snowed in during Jan–Feb. Veleta (3,300 m) is almost certainly blocked Jan–Apr. Plan high climbs for March or later.

## Q2: Other strong candidates for Jan–May

### Takeaway
Sardinia (spring) and Murcia/Sierra Espuña are the most credible additions. Ibiza off-season gets a mention. Murcia, Istria, the Peloponnese, Rhodes, Gozo, Eilat and Oman could not be verified in this round.

### Cited Findings
- Sardinia: spring and autumn are the best seasons. The interior is lightly trafficked. Gennargentu routes collect 2,000 m+ without one signature pass, with long but rarely steep climbs. Bases are Alghero, Nuoro or Cagliari. There is a car-free road through Gennargentu — [procyclinguk](https://procyclinguk.com/cycling-in-sardinia-coastal-roads-wild-interiors-and-why-it-deserves-a-place-on-your-riding-list/); [Rouvy](https://rouvy.com/blog/sardinia-cycling-routes); [tour-magazin](https://www.tour-magazin.de/en/tours/italy/italy-cycling-tours-in-sardinia/)
- Ibiza off-peak: quiet roads, rolling hills, 15–18 °C in winter — [muchbetteradventures](https://www.muchbetteradventures.com/magazine/winter-cycling-destinations-europe/)
- Malta is listed among the less common winter options — [Rouvy](https://rouvy.com/blog/best-cycling-holidays-europe)
- Murcia/Sierra Espuña: my search found no Espuña-specific source, only general Spanish camp context — [brujulabike](https://en.brujulabike.com/spain-favourite-destination-professional-cycling-teams/)

### Inferences
- Sardinia is a strong April–May candidate, quieter than Mallorca. Jan–Feb is likely cool and wet inland.
- Murcia (Sierra Espuña), Alentejo/Algarve combinations and Andalusia generally fill the "dry, mild, empty roads" niche Jan–Apr. Murcia sits next to Costa Blanca and Almería.
- Istria, the Peloponnese, Gran Sasso and Rhodes are likely April–May only. Gran Sasso's high roads are likely snowbound until May. Oman and Eilat are warm in winter but involve long flights and desert-highway riding.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on Murcia, Alentejo, Malta/Gozo, Rhodes, Israel/Eilat, Oman, Gran Sasso, the Peloponnese or Istria, because searches returned nothing specific. These need a dedicated follow-up.

## Q3: Known hazards (dogs, traffic culture, closures, left-hand traffic)

### Takeaway
The confirmed hazards are left-hand traffic and rough side roads in Cyprus, poor race-closure discipline in Antalya, roadworks and transfers in the High Atlas, potholes and livestock in Crete, wind in Lanzarote and Fuerteventura, and snow closures on high climbs (Olympus, Roque, Calar Alto, Lasithi, Etna). I found no direct sources on stray dogs.

### Cited Findings
- Cyprus has left-hand traffic and rough, potholed side-road descents — [Cycling UK](https://www.cyclinguk.org/cycle/great-rides-cyprus-season)
- Brief Olympus snow closures in winter and spring 2026 — [PJAMM](https://pjammcycling.com/climb/3817.Mount-Olympus-Cyprus-%28Trimiklini%29)
- Tour of Antalya peloton met oncoming traffic on a dual carriageway — [Stickybottle](https://www.stickybottle.com/races-results/major-mistake-as-pro-race-forced-to-switch-across-grass-verge-due-to-traffic-video-130560)
- Tizi n'Tichka roadworks can make the first part unrideable — [Sportive Breaks](https://sportivebreaks.com/cycling-climbs/tizi-n-tichka/)
- Crete has potholes on steep slopes, livestock gates, and snow on the Lasithi plateau in winter — [Edelweiss](https://www.flyedelweiss.com/at/fr/destinations/tips/road-cycling/crete.html); [Pinkbike](https://www.pinkbike.com/u/apperstudio/blog/discovering-crete.html)
- Roque de los Muchachos has fog, cloud and occasional snow — [PJAMM](https://pjammcycling.com/climb/428.Roque%20de%20Los%20Muchacho)
- The Velefique road has no shoulder — [PJAMM](https://pjammcycling.com/climb/2707.Alto-Velefique)
- Coll de Rates is crowded with cyclists ("motorway for cyclists") — [Yahoo](https://uk.sports.yahoo.com/news/welcome-costa-blanca-pro-cyclings-065647981.html)

### Inferences
- Stray and guard dogs are widely reported anecdotally in Turkey, Morocco, Crete, Cyprus and rural Andalusia. That claim is not sourced here. Carrying a dog-deterrent strategy is sensible.
- In Morocco, a local guide or support vehicle is advisable for 5–6 riders.

### Gaps
- No primary sources on dog incidents or national traffic-culture statistics. No current 2027 road-closure notices.
